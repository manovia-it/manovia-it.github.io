# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

CIS students (roughly 18-25) and their parents, evaluating whether to move to Italy for university. Both are active audiences in roughly equal measure. As of the repositioning below, the site now targets a narrower slice of this audience deliberately: families for whom price is not the deciding factor and who want someone else to own the entire bureaucratic process, rather than the full price-sensitive CIS market. The site now explicitly signals (via the manifesto/filter section) that budget-driven or DIY-inclined visitors are not the target — this is an intentional narrowing, not an oversight.

## Product Purpose

MANÓVIA is a consulting service that guides CIS students (Russia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Belarus, Kyrgyzstan) through admission to Italian universities: choosing a university, preparing documents, the visa process, applying for the DSU scholarship (up to €7,000-8,000/year), and securing a residence permit — delivered legally, under contract, with a registered business and local support in Italy.

The site's single conversion goal is a submitted "profile audit" application (€100, credited toward the package) rather than an open-ended consultation booking — framed as a selective/closed intake rather than a standard lead-gen form.

## Brand Personality — CONFIRMED REPOSITIONING (2026-07-24)

**This is a real, explicitly confirmed business-model and tone change, not a visual-only exercise.** The client supplied a copywriting brief modeled on an elite Ivy League/Oxbridge boutique agency — "the most expensive, uncompromising option on the market," selling status/certainty/privilege rather than affordability, with an explicit audience filter ("who we don't work with"). An earlier pass in this same session had explicitly excluded this positioning (visual-language-only). The client was shown that exclusion, told directly this contradicts the site's original legitimacy-first strategy and the DSU scholarship's actual need-based nature, and **explicitly confirmed they want to proceed anyway, seriously changing prices[-framing] and positioning.** Treat this as the current source of truth; the earlier "boundary" note is superseded.

Two guardrails were kept even under this confirmed pivot, and should stay non-negotiable going forward regardless of further tone requests, because they cross from marketing style into factual/legal risk:

1. **DSU eligibility is never claimed to be universal or income-independent.** DSU is a real, means-tested Italian government scholarship (based on ISEE/family income). Copy may frame it confidently ("Smart Money," "financial asset," "institutional funding channel") but must keep the ISEE/need-based mechanic visible somewhere in the same block — see the "Smart Money" section copy. Erasing this entirely would be a factual misrepresentation of a government program, not a tone choice.
2. **The legal/contract facts stay present, just confidently phrased rather than defensively phrased.** Italian sole-proprietorship registration and contract-based payment are now stated as terse facts ("Договор. Точка.", "Работаем изнутри системы") instead of justifications ("настоящее ИП, не через схему"), but they are not deleted — this is real consumer-protection substance for an international payment relationship, and dropping it entirely would be a real risk to both the client and the business, independent of brand tone.

Tone is now: confident, terse, declarative, unapologetic. Premium brands state facts, they don't justify them. Strong verbs, short sentences, no hedging language.

## Tone of Voice — Banned Words (confirmed, second brief)

The client supplied a follow-up "final comprehensive brief" reinforcing and tightening the repositioning above. It added an explicit banned-word list for all body copy: **"доступно", "реально", "демократично", "нуждностная", "невысокий доход"**. These were removed from the live copy (the old "why Italy" 4-point benefit section used three of them and was folded into the Smart Money section instead, dropping the "affordable public tuition" framing entirely, since it structurally conflicts with the premium positioning). The DSU/ISEE factual guardrail (see above) still applies — describe the need-based mechanic using ISEE terminology, not the banned words, rather than omitting it.

This same brief also reintroduced real photography (previously removed entirely per an earlier explicit "no photos" instruction) — a moody, high-contrast, verified architectural photograph (Florence Cathedral stairwell, black and white, no people/signage) now anchors the hero. If a future session encounters the earlier "no photography" note, this later instruction supersedes it; confirm with the user before re-removing photography, since this has flip-flopped twice already.

## Package Naming (confirmed)

- **ESSENTIAL** (€3,000, 1 year) — was "Базовый." Same price, same scope.
- **MASTER CYCLE** (€4,000, 2 years) — was "Магистратура."
- **ABSOLUTE** (€5,000, 3 years) — was "Бакалавриат," now the featured/recommended tier.

Prices are unchanged from the original site — only names and surrounding copy changed. Do not silently inflate prices to match "most expensive on the market" rhetoric without an explicit, separate confirmation; that would be a real pricing decision, not a copy decision.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS/tech landing pages** — gradient text, icon-in-circle card grids repeated endlessly, hero-metric templates, tiny uppercase eyebrows on every section.
- **Cold corporate/legal/immigration-law sites** — sterile navy-and-gray, bureaucratic, form-heavy.
- **Cheesy tourist/travel-agency sites** — stock beach photography, "DREAM VACATION" energy.
- (New, from the repositioning) **Apologetic/defensive legitimacy-proving copy** — the site no longer over-explains why it's trustworthy ("не через схему из другой страны," "а не переводом на личную карту"); facts are stated once, tersely, without justifying tone.

## Design Principles

1. **State facts, don't justify them.** The registered business, the contract, the fixed price are stated once, tersely — not defended at length. (Supersedes the earlier "prove legitimacy" framing, which is now considered too apologetic for the confirmed positioning.)
2. **Exactly one accent color, exactly two background tones.** Terracotta is the only color doing active work; ink/paper are the only backgrounds. See DESIGN.md.
3. **Filter, don't chase.** The manifesto/"who we don't work with" section is a deliberate audience-narrowing device, not an oversight — budget-driven and DIY-inclined visitors are explicitly told this isn't for them.
4. **DSU copy stays factually accurate under bolder framing.** "Smart Money" language is fine; claiming income-independent universal eligibility is not.
5. **One clear next action.** Every section ladders up to the "audit application" CTA (€100, credited to contract) — a closed/selective framing, not an open consultation booking.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA: sufficient contrast for body text on both paper (`--paper`/`--ink`) and ink (`--ink`/`--paper`, `--stone-on-ink`) sections, full keyboard navigability (nav, mobile burger menu, FAQ accordions), and reduced-motion alternatives for all scroll/reveal animations (`[data-reveal]`'s `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback).
